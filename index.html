Why Griefcase exists

You don't have to carry everything.

Some thoughts don't have anywhere to go. They're too heavy for a text message, too unfinished for a journal, too honest for a conversation you're not ready to have.

Griefcase isn't therapy, and it isn't trying to fix anything. It's just somewhere to put things down for a while — the anger you didn't say out loud, the grief that doesn't have an occasion, the sentence you keep almost sending.

Some thoughts aren't meant to be solved. Some days you just need somewhere to put them.

You are allowed to feel this. You don't have to explain it to anyone — not even to us.

— Griefcase
Where this is headed

Presence first. Solutions only if you want them.

Griefcase today is exactly what you're using right now: a private space to write and let go, held only in your own browser. The thinking behind it is bigger than that, and it's honest to say so.

Griefcase started from lived grief — from noticing that most people don't want a diagnosis on day one. They want to be heard before anything else. Support that adapts to a person, instead of asking the person to adapt to a system.

The longer-term idea is a layered space that only ever goes as deep as you choose: reflective writing and listening; optional peer support; trained volunteers; and, when it's genuinely wanted, licensed professionals. Every step up would be something you choose — never something a system decides for you.

Worth knowing

Therapist, counselor, psychologist, psychiatrist — what's the difference?

Research into mental health in India keeps finding the same thing: most people can't tell these apart, and that confusion alone stops people from getting help. No pressure to use any of them — just plain definitions.

If you're not sure where to start, a general physician or a counselor is usually a reasonable first stop — they can point you toward the right kind of specialist if you need one.

Privacy & safety

Your thoughts belong to you.

Private by design

In this version of Griefcase, what you write is stored only in your own browser, on this device. It isn't sent to a server, and we don't read it.

Anonymous by default

There's no account, no email, no name required. There's nothing here that identifies you unless you choose to add it yourself.

Yours to keep or delete

You can clear anything you've left here at any time. Closing or clearing your browser will remove it too — nothing is kept anywhere else.

Griefcase is a space for private expression and reflection. It isn't therapy, medical care, or crisis support, and it isn't a substitute for professional help.

If you're in immediate danger

If you or someone you know may be in immediate danger, please contact your local emergency number, or reach a crisis line where you live. findahelpline.com lists helplines by country if you're not sure where to start.

About

A room, not a solution.

Griefcase was built on a simple idea: not every thought needs an audience, an answer, or a fix. Sometimes it just needs somewhere to exist outside of you for a while.

We're not a therapy platform, a social network, or a productivity app. We're closer to a drawer, a shoebox, a case you can close. A small, private container for the things you're carrying.

If Griefcase gives you five quieter minutes in a hard day, it's done its job.

Whenever you're ready.

No sign-up, no streak to keep, no wrong time. Today, tonight, or three years from now — this stays exactly as quiet as you need it to be.
